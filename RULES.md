# Nyog'Sothep — Rules

*"Nyog'Sothep ou les brumes de l'Immonde"* — a board game by Renlo Sublett, published as an insert
in *Jeux & Stratégie* (1988). Translated and condensed from the original French rules (pages 62–64 and 73).
The original French names are given in parentheses where they appear on the counters.

## Background

May 1921, New England. Professor S. Petersen, an ethnologist at the university of Providence, has disappeared.
The Investigators learned that he left for his cottage on Nantucket island. Notes he left behind show that
he is a direct descendant of Margret Petersen, burned for witchcraft in 1688. He is being driven by his lineage
to finish her work: summon to Earth the unspeakable creature **Nyog'Sothep, the Foul Fog-One**
(*l'Immonde Brumeux*). The Investigators must stop the Professor before it is too late.

## Material

- The board: a map of part of New England (Massachusetts and Rhode Island), showing cities, roads,
  sea zones, ports (in green) and state borders (dotted lines).
- Counters:

| Counter                                      | Qty |
|----------------------------------------------|----:|
| Prof. Petersen (*Pr. Petersen*)              | 1   |
| Nyog'Sothep                                  | 1   |
| Investigator (*Investigateur*)               | 6   |
| Fanatics (*Fanatique*)                       | 10  |
| Ghouls (*Goule*)                             | 8   |
| Deep Ones (*Profond*)                        | 8   |
| Things of the Mist (*Choses de la Brume*)    | 3   |
| Inhabitants (*Habitants*)                    | 4   |
| Dreams (*Rêves*)                             | 6   |
| Flying Horror (*Horreur volante*)            | 1   |
| Teleportation (*Téléportation*)              | 2   |
| Storm (*Tempête*)                            | 2   |
| Destroyed city (*Ville détruite*)            | 10  |
| Weapon (*Arme*)                              | 6   |
| Protective sign (*Signe protecteur*)         | 6   |
| Fanatic medallion (*Médaillon de Fanatique*) | 6   |

- At least three six-sided dice, and a screen for the Professor (a game master screen, a folder or a big book).

## Goal of the game

- **Professor Petersen**: gather devoted Fanatics in at least **five different cities**, so he can summon
  Nyog'Sothep and make it appear.
- **The Investigators**: kill the Professor or the Fanatics, prevent the arrival of Nyog'Sothep, or banish it.

## Setup (2 to 7 players)

One player plays Professor Petersen (by agreement or by drawing lots). The others play the Investigators:

- 2 players: the Professor's opponent controls **four** Investigators;
- 3 or 4 players: each opponent controls **two** Investigators;
- 5 to 7 players: each opponent controls **one** Investigator.

Undistributed Investigator counters are set aside and are not used. The Professor takes his own counter
and the Nyog'Sothep counter.

Each player takes a sheet of paper. The Professor keeps his **route sheet** (*feuille de route*) and his
hit points on it. The other players keep track of the sanity points of each of their Investigators.

### Investigators and sanity

At the start of the game, for each Investigator, the player rolls **3d6 + 3**: this is the Investigator's
**sanity (SAN)**.

During the game, Investigators risk losing SAN. An Investigator who loses all their SAN goes mad and becomes
a **Fanatic**: their counter is replaced, where it stands, by a Fanatics counter. It now counts among the
Fanatics the Professor must gather to summon Nyog'Sothep. If all of a player's Investigators have become
Fanatics, that player is eliminated.

An Investigator may always decide, when it is time to move, to **stay in place to consult a psychoanalyst**:
they gain **1d6 SAN**. There is no upper limit to SAN. The character does nothing else that turn. This option
is not available when the Investigator is forced to stay in place (because they lose their turn).

The Investigators start in **Nantucket**, on the island of the same name.

### Professor S. Petersen

The Professor starts with **14 hit points** and can never regain any. If he loses them all, he dies and the
game ends.

Before the game starts, the Professor secretly chooses:

1. the city he starts from;
2. the city where he intends to make Nyog'Sothep appear. He writes it on a piece of paper, folds it and places
   it in plain sight beside the board. It will serve as proof later.

The Professor then rolls **2d6 four times** on **Table 1** and takes the four corresponding counters.

## Sequence of play

A game round is:

1. The Professor plays (moves, may place a counter, draws a new counter).
2. Each Investigator player in turn, starting with the player on the Professor's left and going clockwise,
   moves their Investigator(s) and then resolves the action phase.
3. When the table round is complete, the Professor plays again, and so on.

### The Professor's turn

Each turn, the Professor either stays in the same city or moves to a neighbouring city connected by a road
(or moves by sea, see below).

Secretly, the Professor:

1. writes on his route sheet the city where he arrives;
2. if he wishes, places beside the city name **one** of the counters he holds;
3. writes these two pieces of information on a small piece of paper (a **stage note**, *note d'étape*), folds
   it and places it beside the board. The stage notes, kept in chronological order, serve as proof later.

The Professor's turn ends with a **2d6 roll on Table 1** to receive a new counter. He may **never hold more than
five counters**: if he has six after the draw, he discards one of his choice (without showing it).

> **Example:** the Professor is in Concord with five counters (2 Dreams, 1 Fanatics, 1 Ghouls, 1 Deep Ones).
> He decides to go to Boston and secretly notes the city name on his route sheet. He decides to place a group of
> Fanatics there: he places the counter beside the city name on his sheet, behind his screen. On a piece of
> paper he writes "Boston = Fanatics", folds it and places it beside the board. He then rolls 2d6 and gets
> 4 + 2 = 6: he takes an Inhabitants counter. Since he now holds only four counters, he keeps it. Had he not
> placed a counter in Boston, he would have held six and would have had to discard one.

### Movement

**Professor meeting an Investigator.** When the Professor arrives in a city containing an Investigator, he must
announce it, without saying which Investigator. He then rolls **1d6**: on **1 or 2** he must reveal himself and
fight the Investigator; otherwise he says nothing more. He may also, without rolling, choose to reveal himself
and attack.

**Investigators** move each turn the same way as the Professor: from one city to a neighbouring city connected
by a road, or they stay in place.

**Sea movement** (Investigators and Professor):

- first turn: the character embarks in a port and moves into the adjacent sea zone;
- each following turn: the character moves one sea zone (e.g. from Cape Cod Bay to Massachusetts Bay) or lands
  in a port of the current zone.

Ports are shown in green on the map.

**State borders** (dotted lines on the map). An Investigator who wants to cross a border must go through some
formalities and rolls **1d6**:

| 1d6 | Result |
|-----|--------|
| 1–4 | The Investigator continues their move. |
| 5   | The Investigator stays where they are, but can cross the border without trouble next turn. |
| 6   | The Investigator stays where they are and cannot cross the border at the same place next turn. |

The Professor crosses borders without any problem.

### Action phase

Once the Professor has played and the Investigators have moved, the player on the Professor's left asks him
whether there is an **encounter** in the city where the Investigator is (or has just arrived). There is an
encounter if the Professor has, at any point, placed a counter in that city.

- If there is one, the encounter is resolved (see **Counters**).
- If there is none, or if the encounter allows it, the Investigator draws an **event** on **Table 2 or Table 3**,
  their choice. The table must be chosen **before** rolling:
  - Investigator holding a protective sign **and** a Fanatic medallion: roll **3d6**;
  - Investigator holding only a protective sign: roll **2d6**;
  - otherwise: roll **1d6**.

  The player immediately applies the result.

> **Note:** if a character is sent to another city by an event, they do not roll again there and suffer
> no encounter.

If, after moving and resolving the consequences, the Investigator ends up in the **same city as the Professor**,
the Professor must announce it. **Combat is unavoidable.**

The player's turn is then over, and the next player (on their left) plays the same way.

## Counters

When an encounter occurs, the Professor places the relevant counter on the board, in the corresponding city.

**Ghouls** (*Goules*) — degenerate, necrophagous creatures.
- Without weapon or protective sign: the Investigator loses **4 SAN**.
- With a weapon: the Ghouls are eliminated (the counter goes back to the reserve: it rejoins the counters the
  Professor can draw on Table 1), but the player rolls 1d6: on 1–4 they lose **3 SAN**, on 5–6 only **2 SAN**.
- A protective sign reduces the SAN loss by one point, in every case (without a weapon: 3 SAN instead of 4;
  with a weapon: 2 SAN instead of 3 on 1–4, 1 SAN instead of 2 on 5–6).
- If the Ghouls were not eliminated by a weapon, the Professor puts them back on his route sheet, in the
  corresponding city.

**Deep Ones** (*Profonds*) — semi-aquatic, nauseating beings. The Professor can only place them in a **port**.
- Without a protective sign: the Investigator loses **3 SAN**, and the counter is put back on the Professor's
  route sheet.
- With a protective sign: the Investigator loses only **1 SAN**; the Deep Ones counter goes back to the reserve,
  but the Investigator loses their protective sign.

**Fanatics** (*Fanatiques*)
- Without a weapon: the Investigator is sent back to the city they just left, and the Fanatics stay in place
  (the Professor puts the counter back on his route sheet).
- With a weapon: the player rolls 1d6, **+1 if they hold a medallion**:
  - 1–5: the Investigator is pushed back, as above;
  - 6: the Investigator kills the Fanatics (counter permanently removed from the game) but loses **2 SAN**;
  - 7: the Investigator kills the Fanatics without losing SAN.
- Killing Fanatics gives the Investigator a **medallion** if they don't already have one (they cannot have more
  than one).

**Things of the Mist** (*Choses de la Brume*) — indescribable entities that can only live in fog; Nyog'Sothep
likes to be heralded by them. The Investigator spends their **next turn wandering** and loses **1 SAN**.
The counter goes back to the reserve.

**Inhabitants** (*Habitants*) — exasperated by the Fanatics' rowdy doings, they run out of town any Investigator
wearing a **medallion**. The Investigator loses the medallion and must return to the city they came from. They
cannot go back to the city they were chased from on the following turn. The counter goes back to the reserve.
An Investigator without a medallion is not affected: nothing happens.

**Dreams** (*Rêves*) — through his occult powers, the Professor sends the Investigator, in their sleep, into the
Dreamlands. Their strange adventures there cost them **2 SAN**. The counter goes back to the reserve.

**Storm** (*Tempête*) — keeps the Investigator stuck in the city for **two turns**. The counter is permanently
removed.

**Flying Horror** (*Horreur volante*) — this invisible creature lets the Professor travel paranormally. If he
holds this counter, he may announce, when he moves, that he uses it: he then moves secretly **anywhere within a
radius of three cities**. The counter is permanently removed.

**Teleportation** (*Téléportation*) — to use this spell, the Professor must be **less than three cities away**
from an Investigator. He then moves that Investigator **one or two cities**. The Investigator manipulated by this
occult force loses **2 SAN**.

> Flying Horror and Teleportation are not placed in cities: the Professor uses them before his move, if he holds
> them. After use, the counter is permanently removed.

**Nyog'Sothep** — the Foul Fog-One appears in thick supernatural fogs. Occult books describe it as not very
intelligent but aggressive.

To summon it, Professor Petersen must:
- have placed a **Fanatics counter in at least five cities**, and
- be himself **less than three cities away** from the city chosen for its appearance.

When these conditions are met, he may announce the **summoning**: he reveals the Fanatics and himself on the
board, and shows, for verification, the paper on which he wrote the appearance city, as well as the stage notes
recording the Fanatics.

He places the Nyog'Sothep counter in the chosen city, which is **immediately destroyed**: a Destroyed city counter
is placed there too. Nyog'Sothep cannot move until the Professor has joined it. From then on, both counters move
together. Each turn the Professor rolls **1d6**: on **1 or 2** they don't move; otherwise they move **one city**.
Every city they enter is destroyed: a Destroyed city counter is placed there, and Investigators can no longer
pass through it.

## Fighting Nyog'Sothep

An Investigator in the same city as the Foul Fog-One **dies immediately** if they have no protective sign.
Otherwise they lose **4 SAN** on their first encounter with it, and **2 SAN** on each following one.

### The banishment spell

At least **three Investigators** must know the **banishment spell** (learned through Tables 2 and 3) and gather
in the **same city**. As soon as they do, Nyog'Sothep has only one goal: go there and annihilate these
presumptuous wretches. The Professor and Nyog'Sothep must head for that city by the **shortest path**, whatever
it is.

As soon as the Foul One arrives, the Investigators cast the spell. Each loses 4 or 2 SAN (from seeing the Foul
One, see above). One Investigator rolls **1d6** for the group and **subtracts the number of participants**:

| Result     | Effect |
|------------|--------|
| 3          | Each participant loses another **4 SAN**; the spell fails and Nyog'Sothep is free. |
| 2          | Each participant loses another **2 SAN**; the spell fails and Nyog'Sothep is free. |
| 1 or 0     | The spell fails and Nyog'Sothep is free. |
| −1         | The spell partially works: the players take Nyog'Sothep and place it in the city of their choice. |
| −2 or less | **Victory!** Nyog'Sothep is cast back into the bottomless abyss for many generations. The game ends. |

If the spell failed or only partially worked, Nyog'Sothep carries on as usual and tries to kill or drive mad all
the Investigators. The game continues until there are not enough Investigators left to cast the spell (at least
three).

## Fighting the Professor

When an Investigator meets the Professor, the outcome depends on what they hold:

- **Nothing**: the Investigator loses **4 SAN** and the Professor moves them **two cities** of his choice
  (except at sea).
- **A weapon**: the player rolls **1d6**: on 1 nothing happens; on 2–5 the Professor loses **2 hit points**;
  on 6 he loses **3**. The Professor then **retreats one city** (without placing a counter there).
- **Only a protective sign**: the Investigator loses only **2 SAN** and the Professor is **pushed back one city**
  (of the Professor's choice). The Investigator loses their protective sign. If they prefer, they can instead
  attack with a weapon without using their protective sign, but they cannot combine both.
- **A Fanatic medallion**: the character is mistaken for a Fanatic and nothing happens (unless they decide to
  attack Professor Petersen).

Killing the Professor before Nyog'Sothep appears ends the game.

## End of the game

**The Professor wins** if he managed to make Nyog'Sothep appear and **fewer than three Investigators remain**.
The Foul Fog-One can no longer be banished, and its dreadful reign will be endless.

**The Investigators win** if they **kill the Professor before Nyog'Sothep arrives**, or if they **banish the Foul
Fog-One**. This victory is collective. The Investigators can still be ranked against each other, for a single game
or across several games, with the following scoring:

| Achievement | Points |
|-------------|-------:|
| Per hit point taken from the Professor | +2 |
| Per group of Fanatics killed | +3 |
| Each Investigator who took part in a successful banishment of Nyog'Sothep | +5 |
| Per SAN point left at the end of the game | +1 |
| Having become a Fanatic | −5 |
| Per city destroyed by Nyog'Sothep | −2 per Investigator |

## Notes on unclear points in the original

This implementation is a two-player game: the human leads the four Investigators and the computer plays
Professor Petersen. Where the rules are silent or ambiguous, it reads them as follows.

### Setup and turn order

- **Investigators:** always four (the two-player setup). Four of the six counters are drawn at random for
  each game; the other two are not used.
- **Turn order:** the professor plays; then all four investigators move; then the action phase of each
  investigator is resolved in turn (encounter, else event, then the unavoidable fight). A "play again" event
  gives that investigator another move at once, then a whole new action phase (encounter included).
- **Hand limit:** the professor always draws on Table 1, then discards down to five if he holds six.
- **Visible information:** the human sees the professor's hit points (the investigators dealt the damage),
  never the size of his hand.

### The map

- **Ports:** the board shows 18 green ports. Westerly and Gloucester are ports, although the old data of the
  Rails application did not mark them. Gloucester, Edgartown and Nantucket have no road: only boats reach them.
- **Forks and crossings:** a road fork or crossing without a city joins every road meeting there, so the
  cities at their ends are neighbours (for example Greenbush and Bridgewater Junction, Woonsocket and Bristol,
  Apponaug and Westerly). Such a road crosses a state border when any of its parts does (Oxford–Pascoag,
  Attleboro–Bristol and Taunton–Bristol cross one).
- **"Less than three cities away"** (Nyog'Sothep's summoning, Teleportation) means at most two steps, by road
  or by sea.
- **At sea:** an investigator who ends their move in a sea zone has no action phase (no event, no fight), and
  the professor and an investigator in the same sea zone do not meet. Nobody consults a psychoanalyst at sea.
- **Borders:** on a 5 the investigator may cross any border without a roll on the next turn; on a 6 they may
  not cross into that same city on the next turn.

### Counters

- **Stacking:** at most one counter per city, hidden or discovered. The professor may place a counter in the
  city he stays in, provided it holds none. Deep Ones go in ports only.
- **Events after an encounter:** the encounter "allows" an event when the investigator overcomes it and stays:
  Ghouls killed with a weapon, Deep Ones repelled with the sign, Fanatics killed, Inhabitants meeting someone
  without a medallion. Every other encounter ends the action phase.
- **Inhabitants** do nothing to an investigator without a medallion and stay in the city, face up.
- **Lost turns** (psychoanalyst event, Things of the Mist, Storm): the investigator neither moves nor draws an
  encounter or an event; meeting the professor still means a fight. Things of the Mist cost 1 SAN at once and
  the next turn; the Storm costs the next two turns.
- **Broken car:** on the next turn the investigator either takes to the sea or stays and loses the turn.
- **Pushed back, go back:** the investigator returns to the place their last move left, which may be a sea
  zone; nothing happens when that city has been destroyed.
- **Teleportation** targets an investigator standing in a city at most two steps from the professor and moves
  them one or two cities by road; it costs them 2 SAN. Teleportation and the Flying Horror are announced, so
  they tell the investigators something about the professor's position.

### Fighting the professor

- **Meeting on arrival:** the professor announces it only when he moves into a city holding investigators; the
  human then knows he is in one of the cities where an investigator stands. On a 1 or 2 he must reveal himself
  and fight one of them, of his choice; otherwise he may choose to. Investigators left in his city fight him
  in their own action phase.
- **Choices:** an investigator holding several items chooses: weapon, protective sign, or (with a medallion)
  passing for a Fanatic. Someone holding nothing loses 4 SAN and is carried two cities by road (one when no
  city lies two steps away).
- **Retreat and push-back:** the professor moves one step of his choice, by road or to sea, and places
  nothing there.
- **After the summoning** the professor stays with Nyog'Sothep and no longer fights the investigators.

### Nyog'Sothep

- **Summoning:** the five cities count the Fanatics counters in place at that moment, including those raised
  by investigators gone mad. Once Nyog'Sothep is out, the professor stays in plain sight.
- **Investigator going mad:** they become a Fanatics counter, face up, where they stand. A counter already in
  that city goes back to the reserve, so the stacking rule has no exception.
- **Killed by Nyog'Sothep:** an investigator without a protective sign who meets it is dead: no Fanatics
  counter and no −5 "became a Fanatic" score. They count as lost for the "fewer than three Investigators" check.
- **Movement:** Nyog'Sothep and the professor move one step together, by road or by sea like the professor (so
  that they can reach investigators gathered on an island); only the cities they enter are destroyed, and
  nobody is met at sea. Investigators may no longer enter or cross a destroyed city; the professor and
  Nyog'Sothep can.
- **Banishment:** as soon as three investigators knowing the spell stand in the same city, Nyog'Sothep heads
  there by the shortest path. The spell is cast when it enters a city holding three spell holders, after their
  SAN loss for meeting it. On −1 the human chooses the city where Nyog'Sothep goes; it destroys that city and
  waits there for the professor to join it again.
- **Fewer than three investigators before the summoning:** the game goes on, but the summoning then wins at
  once since Nyog'Sothep can no longer be banished. The game is lost at once only when all four investigators
  are out (gone mad or dead).

### Answers to the investigators

- When the professor must name cities "one of which is his position", the answer is checked: one named city is
  always his real position, and he says so when he is at sea. The same goes for "two cities, one of which
  holds Fanatics"; with no Fanatics in place he says that there are none.
- "Encounters in two cities" is answered for each city; "is Nyog'Sothep to appear in one of them" is answered
  for the pair, without saying which.
- Table 3 #13 reveals the professor's position at once and the destination of his next move.
